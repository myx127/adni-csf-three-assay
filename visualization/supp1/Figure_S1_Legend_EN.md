**Supplementary Figure S1. Study design, participant selection, and analytical framework.**

This paired ADNI study compared three CSF proteomic assays to examine shared p-tau181 associations, cognitive associations and the effects of p-tau181 adjustment.

(A) NULISA, SomaScan and TMT-MS measurements were matched by participant and exact CSF collection date. One sampling visit was included for each of 571 participants. Usable same-day Elecsys p-tau181 measurements (8–120 pg/mL) were available for 567. Seven participants lacked valid age information, leaving 560 with p-tau181, age and sex available; education and APOE status were also complete. Of these, 557 had a memory assessment within 90 days before or after CSF and at least one later assessment more than 90 days after CSF and within five years. For each protein and outcome, the three assay models used the same participants with complete measurements; sample sizes therefore varied across analyses. Matching by collection date did not confirm use of the same physical aliquot.

(B) Seventy protein targets were reported in all three datasets. Twenty-six had multiple SomaScan matches and one had a combined protein annotation, leaving 43 with an unambiguous match. Before testing protein–p-tau181 associations, proteins were assessed for NULISA detectability (≥80% in both the matched cohort and the sample for that protein), complete observations (≥200), residual degrees of freedom (≥100), and representation in at least ten technical batches for each assay. Eight did not meet one or more requirements. MAPT was analyzed separately among the 35 remaining proteins, leaving 34 for the main analyses.

(C) All 34 proteins were evaluated for shared p-tau181 associations and cognitive associations. Annual change in ADNI-MEM was the primary cognitive outcome; baseline ADNI-MEM and annual change in ADNI-EF were secondary outcomes. Fourteen proteins showed shared p-tau181 associations after BY correction and were highlighted in the clinical interpretation. Analyses of cognitive associations and multiple-testing correction included all 34 proteins. NPTX2, TAFA5 and TIMP3 were selected for exploratory analyses after their memory associations differed across assays. Nine models examined p-tau181 adjustment, alternative functional forms and background protein scores. Paired tests assessed whether differences between assays changed after adjustment, using the same participants, observations and protein standardization for each protein. Sensitivity analyses examined covariate adjustment, follow-up criteria, participant weighting and technical batch influence; simulations assessed false-positive rates and power.

Details are provided in Supplementary Methods Sections 1–8, Table M1 and Supplementary Tables S1–S8, S10–S12, S14–S15.

Abbreviations: ADNI, Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative; ADNI-EF, ADNI executive function composite; ADNI-MEM, ADNI memory composite; APOE, apolipoprotein E gene; BY, Benjamini–Yekutieli; CSF, cerebrospinal fluid; MAPT, microtubule-associated protein tau; p-tau181, phosphorylated tau 181; TMT-MS, tandem mass tag mass spectrometry.
