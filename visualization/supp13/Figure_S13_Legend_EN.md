**Supplementary Figure S13. Regression relationships underlying changes in memory associations after p-tau181 adjustment.**

The three candidates are NPTX2 (n=553), TAFA5 (n=557) and TIMP3 (n=552). Candidate selection used the preceding memory-trajectory outcome; these panels provide exploratory explanations within the same samples. Eligible ADNI-MEM assessments were within five years after CSF collection, with baseline permitted up to 90 days before collection. All models for each protein retained identical participants, observations, within-person time-information weights and fixed protein standard deviations (SDs).

(A) Weighted residual Pearson correlations for protein with log₂ p-tau181, protein with memory slope, and log₂ p-tau181 with memory slope, after adjustment for the common covariates. The p-tau181–memory correlation is common across assays within each protein because the participant sample, outcome and weights are identical. Values are rounded to three decimals; the color scale spans −1 to 1.

(B) Open markers show coefficients without p-tau181 adjustment; filled markers show coefficients after linear log₂ p-tau181 adjustment, without background scores. Circles, squares and diamonds represent NULISA, SomaScan and TMT-MS, respectively. Lines join two regression estimates for the same protein and assay. Units are annual ADNI-MEM score change per fixed assay-specific protein residual SD. Positive coefficients indicate more favorable memory change, including less decline. Point estimates illustrate the regression relationship; uncertainty and direct paired-change tests are presented in Supplementary Figs. S11–S12.

The common covariates were age, sex, education, APOE ε4 carrier status, NULISA plate, SomaScan plate, TMT plex and ADNI Elecsys data release. Residual relationships use the time-information-weighted slope representation of participant fixed-effect models; all covariates and added p-tau181/background terms interacted with time. The Frisch–Waugh–Lovell identity relates the adjusted coefficient to the protein–memory cross-product after subtracting the p-tau181-associated component, divided by the remaining protein sum of squares. Positive protein–p-tau181 correlations and negative p-tau181–memory correlations therefore change both the numerator and denominator while the reporting scale stays fixed.

(C) Cells show the percentage of weighted residual protein sum of squares retained in each of the nine adjustment models. Every cell uses the same protein-and-assay-specific reference denominator: the model containing common covariates without p-tau181 or background scores. “None” denotes that reference. After linear log₂ p-tau181 adjustment alone, the retained fraction is 1 − r², using the first correlation column in Panel A; its reciprocal is the corresponding variance inflation factor. Spline and background-model fractions use their respective residual sums of squares. Values are rounded to one decimal place; colors span 0–100% without truncation. Two scores refer to SomaScan and TMT-MS backgrounds; three additionally include NULISA.

The retained proportion summarizes observed protein variation remaining after adjustment. Background scores can include biological and technical variation. Full results are provided in Supplementary Table S16; definitions and derivations are in Supplementary Methods Sections 6–7, Table M1 and Appendix D.

Abbreviations: ADNI-MEM, Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative memory composite; APOE, apolipoprotein E gene; CSF, cerebrospinal fluid; SD, standard deviation; TMT-MS, tandem mass tag mass spectrometry.
